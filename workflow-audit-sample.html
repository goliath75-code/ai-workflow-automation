The problem

Good leads were waiting too long.

Website inquiries landed in a shared inbox. Someone copied details into the CRM, decided who owned the lead, and wrote the first response when time allowed.

What we found

The delay was not one person’s fault.

  • Lead data was entered twice.
  • Ownership was unclear after hours.
  • Follow-up depended on someone noticing the inbox.
  • There was no simple way to see what had been missed.

Risk notes

What should not be automated blindly

  • Pricing, commitments, and exception handling still need a person.
  • Any customer-facing message needs an approved tone and escalation path.
  • CRM ownership rules must be agreed before automation starts.
Recommended first build

Automate the handoff, not the judgment.

  • Create or match the CRM record automatically.
  • Route the lead using agreed rules and create a clear task.
  • Prepare a context-aware response for review or approved sending.
  • Alert the owner when the lead has not been handled in time.

Recommendation

Worth automating after a short rules-and-approval review. The biggest gain is faster, more consistent first response—not replacing the person who handles the customer conversation.
